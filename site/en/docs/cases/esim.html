eSIM: SwiftUI, payments, 3DS and service management

2025–2026 · OneTwoTrip / iOS

Built the eSIM journey from catalogue to service management, with a particular focus on SwiftUI checkout, REST API integration, 3DS, promo codes and payment-timer lifecycle.

View screens and states · Full gallery

Context

Travelers need a straightforward way to buy mobile data and return to their eSIM during a trip. This journey combines a product catalog, authentication, payments, service state, device limitations and network availability. I worked on both the initial launch and subsequent product iterations.

My contribution

  • Implemented entry points from the home screen and deep links, coverage selection, search, plan lists and descriptions. Integrated product screens with the shared modular infrastructure.
  • Worked on the payment screen and its backend integration: embedded authentication, bank and saved cards, 3DS, status polling, loading and error states. Added contact entry and validation, including users without an email address.
  • Developed My eSIMs, order details, balance, top-ups and navigation to setup. Extended the journey with discounts, promo codes, refreshed offers after timer expiry and alternative coverage names in search.
  • Contributed to offline scenarios, setup guidance and eSIM offers within flight orders. Automatic offers accounted for departure time and reopening an order.
  • Added analytics events for the widget, purchase, eSIM list and top-ups; developed unit, snapshot and UI tests.

Technologies

Swift, SwiftUI + UIKit / UIHostingController, TCA, Combine, Swift Concurrency / async-await, Swinject, REST / Codable, 3DS, Tuist, XCTest / SnapshotTesting / XCUITest.

Engineering decisions

  • Screen state is separate from navigation: the reducer handles state and effects, PropsBuilder prepares presentation, and the router/coordinator handles navigation and payment orchestration.
  • Purchase and top-up have different inputs and rules: purchase requires contacts, while top-up uses an existing eSIM identifier. The module contract preserves that distinction.
  • The payment timer uses an absolute deadline: ticks stop in the background and the remaining time is recalculated on return. This avoids incorrect payment time after the device locks.
  • Retaining an already loaded list after connectivity is lost refers to the open screen's memory, not a complete persistent offline store.

Quality and checks

  • Separate work covered purchase and My eSIMs unit tests, component snapshots, and UI checks for entry points, payment and service management.
  • Unit tests cover 3DS requests, stale promo-validation responses, partial and full discounts, resuming before and after the deadline, and late timer ticks. Snapshot tests capture the price breakdown; UI tests cover purchase, top-up and promo codes.

Outcome and scope

A connected iOS journey for buying and managing eSIMs, extended through further iterations after launch.

A closer look at the journey

  1. Prepare checkout. Load the plan and payment methods, validate purchase contacts or provide the eSIM identifier for a top-up.
  2. Resolve the total. Apply the discount and validated promo code. A fully covered total follows a separate path without card selection.
  3. Process payment. Create the order and continue payment; open 3DS when required, then poll for status.
  4. Present the result. Present success based on the service response; handle cancellation, failure and expiry as separate outcomes.

A simplified route through one journey. Error branches and additional actions are discussed below.

SwiftUI + TCA: a form inside a payment journey

The screen combines plan details, contacts, a payment method, a promo code and the total. I worked on the UI, validation and backend integration. SwiftUI is hosted in UIHostingController; TCA manages state and asynchronous effects, a PropsBuilder produces presentation data, and a router handles navigation. Purchase and top-up share mechanisms but require different data: a top-up uses the existing eSIM ICCID and omits the purchase-contact section.

3DS and API-confirmed completion

Developed the chain of order creation, payment continuation, 3DS and result polling. The service can confirm payment immediately or request bank verification. After 3DS, the client polls for the result; closing verification does not stand in for purchase confirmation. Cancelling 3DS clears the processing state, while failures lead to an actionable next step. Other payment integrations and the shared waiting UI.

Promo codes: asynchronous validation and consistent pricing

Added promo-code support for purchases and top-ups. A response is accepted only for the current request ID and pricing context, preventing a stale validation result from changing the user’s new selection. Payment is unavailable during validation, and the total and breakdown reflect the confirmed discount. Full coverage needs a cardless branch that still creates and confirms the order. The snapshot below shows the plan discount, promo discount and final total separately.

Timer, screen lock and app resumption

Fixed timer behaviour after screen lock and backgrounding. Remaining time is derived from an absolute deadline; ticks are cancelled while inactive and recalculated on resumption. A late tick must not present an expiry sheet in the background or present it twice. Unit tests use controlled time for these transitions; UI checks also cover refreshing offers after checkout expires.
